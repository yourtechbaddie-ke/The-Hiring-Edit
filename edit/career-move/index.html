The art of making a memorable career move.

How to recognize the moment when a new chapter deserves your attention — and move with intention rather than urgency.

The most consequential career decisions rarely arrive with a perfect sense of certainty. More often, they begin as a quiet feeling: that the work which once fit no longer quite does, or that a possibility you would once have ignored now feels worth considering.

Know the difference between restlessness and readiness.

Not every difficult season is a signal to leave. Sometimes the answer is a boundary, a conversation or simply time away from the pace of work. A genuine career move tends to feel different. It creates curiosity alongside discomfort. You begin asking better questions about what you want to learn, who you want to work beside and what kind of life the role would support.

Before you search, define the conditions that would make a move meaningful. Title matters, but so do scope, leadership, culture, flexibility, compensation and the opportunity to build something you can be proud of.

Choose the chapter, not just the job.

A compelling opportunity is more than a list of responsibilities. It is an environment in which your strengths can become more valuable. Look beyond the immediate role description and study the trajectory: what will you be trusted with after six months? Who will influence your thinking? What does exceptional performance look like there?

The strongest moves often make sense in hindsight because they connect several threads at once — experience you already have, capability you want to develop and a direction that feels increasingly like your own.

Make the decision with evidence.

Intuition deserves a seat at the table, but it should not be the only voice. Speak with people who understand the industry. Read the role carefully. Ask direct questions during interviews. Compare the opportunity with the priorities you wrote down before the search began.

And remember that moving forward does not require making your current chapter smaller. A thoughtful career move respects what your existing experience has taught you while making room for what comes next.

The memorable move is rarely the loudest one. It is the one that makes your future feel more deliberate.
